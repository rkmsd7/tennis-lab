Grip

The six tennis grips

Every grip is defined by where the index knuckle and the heel pad of your hand sit on the eight bevels of the handle. Diagrams are drawn for a right-hander looking down the handle from the butt cap; lefties mirror them.

How to read the bevel diagram. Bevel 1 is on top when the racket face is vertical. Count clockwise for a right-hander (anticlockwise for a left-hander). The highlighted bevel is where the base knuckle of your index finger goes; the heel of your hand sits on the same bevel.

How to find each grip in 10 seconds

Continental

Hold the racket on its edge like a hammer, or shake hands with the frame edge-on.

Eastern

Put your palm flat on the strings, slide it down to the handle and close your fingers.

Semi-Western

From Eastern, rotate the hand one bevel under the handle (clockwise for righties).

Western

Lay the racket face-down on the court and pick it up without turning your hand.

Eastern backhand

From Continental, rotate one bevel the other way so the knuckles face the net on the backhand side.

Two-handed

Bottom hand Continental; top hand shakes hands with the racket (Eastern/Semi-Western) directly above it.
